Swoop / new collaborator brief

Recover the lead before it calls someone else.

Swoop is an AI-assisted missed-call text-back product for small home-service businesses. This brief explains the product thesis, the working system, the controlled-pilot plan, and what still stands between today's demo and the first real business.

Executive snapshot

The promise: when a business misses a call, Swoop quickly sends a clearly branded service text, gathers the caller's need in a short conversation, and gives the owner an actionable handoff.

Working

Internal demo

The Node, SQLite, Twilio, and OpenAI path exists. A direct call to the internal toll-free number can ring the owner and trigger the missed-call SMS.

Current objective

Controlled pilot

Prepare a deliberately small, free pilot with concierge onboarding, active monitoring, reversible call forwarding, and direct founder support.

Not ready

External business use

Authentication, production forwarding behavior, local-number compliance, owner alerts, and automated compliance evidence remain unresolved.

Important distinctionThe verified toll-free number is an internal demo and test asset. Real businesses are intended to keep their existing public number and conditionally forward unanswered calls to one dedicated local Twilio number per business.

Vision and product principles

Swoop is built for an owner-operator who is doing the work and answering the phone. A plumber under a sink, an electrician on a ladder, or a cleaner with both hands occupied cannot reliably answer every new-business call. The caller often tries the next company instead of leaving voicemail.

Near-term vision

Make the missed-call moment recoverable. Capture the request, qualify it just enough, identify urgency, and put the owner back in control without requiring the caller to install anything or learn a new channel.

Long-term vision

Become the lightweight customer-response layer for owner-operated service businesses: first capture the lead, then help close it, and eventually support appropriate retention workflows after explicit consent.

Decision filter

Simple

Phone-first, understandable without training, and useful without constant dashboard attention.

Modular

A new business is configuration and data, not a custom code branch.

Affordable

Target price is $29-$49 per business per month, with infrastructure kept lean until demand is proven.

The opportunity

The economic thesis is straightforward: home-service calls are high intent, response time matters, and even one recovered job can cover many months of Swoop. The current planning assumption is that a missed opportunity can be worth roughly $200-$500 and that some owner-operators miss several calls per week.

Why the wedge is attractive

  • The trigger is observable: a call was missed.
  • The caller already initiated contact, so the text is a service response rather than cold marketing.
  • SMS requires no new caller behavior.
  • The value can be explained in jobs recovered, not software activity.
  • A narrow product can remain cheaper and simpler than broad CRM suites.

What still needs proof

  • How many relevant calls target businesses actually miss.
  • What percentage of callers reply to the first text.
  • Whether qualified handoffs become booked jobs.
  • Whether owners respond quickly enough to preserve trust.
  • Whether owners will pay after a free pilot.
These are hypotheses, not traction claimsSwoop has no live pilot or paying customer yet. The first controlled cohort must validate missed-call volume, reply rate, booked jobs, support burden, and willingness to pay.

Intended customer experience

Step 1Familiar callThe caller dials the business's existing public number.
Step 2Normal ringThe business phone behaves normally. Swoop does nothing if answered.
Step 3Conditional forwardOnly a busy or unanswered call forwards to the business's dedicated Twilio number.
Step 4Text and qualifyOne branded text starts a short, one-question-at-a-time service conversation.
Step 5Owner handoffThe owner gets the need, location, urgency, timing preference, and next action.

Swoop must not promise a booked appointment or arrival time unless a reliable integration or the owner confirms it. Safety emergencies stop normal qualification; urgent property issues should use minimal intake and immediate escalation.

Controlled-pilot strategy

Gate 1 is not a scalable SaaS launch. It is a small learning environment with concierge setup, active monitoring, direct support, and reversible forwarding. The working duration hypothesis is three to four weeks; cohort size and success thresholds remain open decisions.

What the pilot should learn

  • Missed calls captured per business
  • Successful first-text and caller reply rates
  • Qualified and urgent handoffs
  • Owner response time and jobs booked
  • Opt-outs, failures, and incorrect AI replies
  • Support time and willingness to continue paying

What is deliberately deferred

  • Automated signup and billing
  • Fully self-service number purchasing
  • Calendar and guaranteed booking integrations
  • Advanced analytics and native mobile apps
  • Multiple roles, CRM integrations, and broad industry scale
  • Complex subscription packaging
