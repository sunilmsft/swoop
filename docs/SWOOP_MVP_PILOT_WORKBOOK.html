Product framing

Swoop helps a small business recover missed opportunities without changing how customers already know and contact the business.

When an existing business number goes unanswered, the call is conditionally forwarded behind the scenes to Swoop. Swoop sends a warm, clearly branded text, gathers useful information through a short multi-turn conversation, and hands the qualified request to the business owner.

Caller

  • Receives a fast acknowledgment after an unanswered call.
  • Understands which business is texting and why.
  • Can describe the need without calling repeatedly.
  • Reaches a human owner when judgment or action is required.
  • Can opt out easily.

Business owner

  • Keeps the existing customer-facing number.
  • Avoids losing customers because a call was missed.
  • Receives a concise, qualified lead rather than a raw notification.
  • Can see what the customer needs and what action to take.
  • Can enable, pause, or disable Swoop without disrupting the public identity.

Swoop operator

  • Onboards and supports a deliberately small pilot cohort.
  • Sees whether each business is functioning.
  • Troubleshoots calls, SMS, AI, and handoff failures.
  • Manages the pilot without routine production-data edits.
  • Learns which steps should become self-service.

Two readiness gates

Gate 1 Controlled Pilot MVP

Safe enough for a small number of selected businesses to use free for approximately 3–4 weeks with concierge onboarding, active monitoring, and direct support from Swoop.

Current objective. The pilot is a learning environment, not a fully scalable SaaS launch.

Gate 2 Repeatable Business MVP

Stable and simple enough to onboard additional businesses through a repeatable process with less manual technical intervention.

Not the current objective. Self-service, automation, and scale should be earned through pilot evidence.

Confirmed decisions

These are working product decisions for the pilot. Revisit only when the stated trigger occurs.

D-001 — Toll-free number remains internal-only Confirmed

Date
August 19, 2026
Decision
The currently verified toll-free number is exclusively for internal development, validation, and testing. It must not be advertised, shared with external testers, prospects, or pilot businesses.
Rationale
Verification required significant time and effort, so it is a protected internal test asset. A tester's device also flagged the number as possible fraud.
Implication
External pilot businesses require separate appropriate Twilio numbers.
Revisit when
Revisit only if the internal testing strategy changes.

D-002 — Pilot supports existing-number forwarding only Confirmed

Date
August 19, 2026
Decision
Pilot businesses retain their current customer-facing number. Only unanswered or busy calls are conditionally forwarded to a dedicated Twilio number. Forwarding must be reversible.
Rationale
Owners should not change Google listings, websites, vehicles, business cards, directories, or existing customer communications.
Implication
A new Twilio number as the publicly advertised number is outside Gate 1 and remains a possible future model.
Revisit when
Pilot evidence shows a public new-number model materially improves outcomes without harming trust.

D-003 — One dedicated Twilio number per pilot business Confirmed

Date
August 19, 2026
Decision
The pilot assumes a 1:1 mapping between each business and its assigned Twilio number. It is a behind-the-scenes routing and texting number, not the advertised number.
Rationale
The mapping identifies business profile, AI instructions, sender identity, owner, dashboard, and handoff destination.
Implication
Shared toll-free or shared sender behavior is not an acceptable pilot architecture.
Revisit when
There is evidence that a different isolation model is equally understandable and safe.

D-004 — Concierge onboarding is acceptable Confirmed

Date
August 19, 2026
Decision
Initial onboarding can be completed manually. Automated signup, purchasing, fully self-service carrier setup, and automated billing are not required for Gate 1.
Rationale
A deliberately small cohort lets Swoop learn the real setup burden before automating it.
Implication
Every manual step must be recorded so future self-service candidates are visible.
Revisit when
Concierge support becomes the limiting factor for pilot quality or cohort growth.

D-005 — Scheduling preference capture is MVP; guaranteed booking is not Confirmed

Date
August 19, 2026
Decision
Swoop may ask for a preferred callback or service window and include it in the owner handoff. It must not claim an appointment is booked unless a reliable integration confirms it.
Rationale
Capturing intent helps owners without creating a false promise.
Implication
Full scheduling integrations are outside the initial pilot. A reliable public scheduling link may later be an optional configured behavior.
Revisit when
A pilot business requests booking often enough to justify a defined integration.

D-006 — Emergency routing is an MVP safety requirement Confirmed

Date
August 19, 2026
Decision
Emergency and urgent service requests are treated differently from routine qualification. Safety emergencies stop normal qualification; urgent property issues use minimal intake and immediate owner escalation.
Rationale
Slow conversational handling can create physical or property risk.
Implication
Swoop must not promise arrival or resolution times the owner has not confirmed. Exact safety language remains an open launch question.
Revisit when
Industry-specific safety review changes the approved routing rules.

Supported pilot architecture

1. Familiar callCustomer calls the existing business number.
2. Normal ringThe business phone rings normally.
3. AnsweredIf answered, Swoop does nothing.
4. No answerProvider conditionally forwards unanswered or busy calls.
5. IdentifyTwilio/Swoop maps the dedicated number to one business.
6. Do not redialSwoop avoids dialing the owner a second time.
7. Record onceOne missed-call event is recorded.
8. Text onceExactly one warm initial text is sent.
9. ConversationCustomer replies through the assigned Twilio number.
10. Qualify or escalateUrgency changes the path.
11. HandoffOwner receives a concise request and takes over.
12. DisableOwner turns off conditional forwarding to pause.

Experience journeys

Caller journey

  • Calls the familiar business number.
  • Receives no confusing second call flow.
  • Gets a warm, identified text explaining the missed call.
  • Answers one short question at a time.
  • Understands when an automated assistant is helping.
  • Can request a human or opt out.
  • Receives special emergency handling.
  • Is not told an appointment is booked unless confirmed.

Business-owner journey

  • Keeps the existing public number.
  • Enables conditional forwarding with guided instructions.
  • Completes a test-call verification before go-live.
  • Sees missed calls, conversation status, need, location, urgency, timing, and next action.
  • Receives immediate intervention notification.
  • Takes over without reading an entire transcript.
  • Can pause or disable the pilot.
  • Sees only that business's data.

Swoop-operator journey

  • Creates the business profile and maps its dedicated number.
  • Provides carrier-specific forwarding instructions.
  • Runs answered and missed-call tests.
  • Verifies first SMS, reply flow, urgency, notification, and opt-out.
  • Monitors health and usage.
  • Troubleshoots call, SMS, AI, and forwarding failures.
  • Pauses or offboards safely.
  • Tracks outcomes and costs.

Onboarding journey

  1. Select and qualify a business.
  2. Complete intake and privacy acknowledgement.
  3. Provision and map one dedicated Twilio number.
  4. Configure business and AI profile.
  5. Set up reversible conditional forwarding.
  6. Run answered-call, missed-call, SMS, reply, urgency, handoff, and opt-out tests.
  7. Set up owner dashboard/access and notification path.
  8. Review pilot expectations and support channel.
  9. Record go-live approval and schedule check-ins.

Offboarding journey

  1. Disable conditional forwarding.
  2. Stop Swoop messaging.
  3. Release or retain the Twilio number according to policy.
  4. Revoke owner access.
  5. Export, delete, or retain data according to the agreed policy.
  6. Collect final feedback and decide continuation.

Urgency architecture

Level 1 — Safety emergency

Examples: gas smell, fire or smoke, electrical danger around water, immediate danger to people.

Expected: stop normal qualification; tell the caller not to wait for a text response; use approved safety language; direct to the appropriate emergency service; alert the owner immediately; do not improvise repair instructions.

Level 2 — Urgent property issue

Examples: burst pipe, active flooding, major leak, complete loss of water, no heat during dangerous weather.

Expected: mark urgent; collect only name, location, and best callback number; alert the owner immediately; confirm escalation without promising arrival time.

Level 3 — Routine request

Examples: slow drain, fixture replacement, estimate, maintenance.

Expected: use the short qualification flow; capture scheduling preference; hand off when enough information exists.

Q-009 Exact safety and emergency language must be approved before pilot launch, potentially per business and industry.

Week 2Conversation and handoff learning.

  1. Keep this file as the single living workbook. Update it in place; do not create versioned copies.
  2. Use stable IDs for decisions (D-###) and open questions (Q-###). Do not reuse an ID.
  3. Use status chips consistently: Confirmed decision Working recommendation Open question Current gap Post-MVP.
  4. When a question closes, add a dated decision with rationale and revisit trigger. Preserve the question's history.
  5. Session history is append-only. Add a dated entry; do not rewrite prior entries to make the story cleaner.
  6. When implementation changes, reconcile the requirement matrix against code and tests. Documentation alone does not make a capability working.
  7. Before a pilot decision, record the evidence, acceptance criterion, and owner responsible for the next step.
