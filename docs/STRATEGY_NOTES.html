1. The core decision: focus on Swoop, pause WelcomeMat repositioning

WelcomeMat Digital's broader vision — connected, discovery-led customer experiences — is compelling but hard to explain cold, and requires the buyer to trust before seeing value. Outreach so far (FB groups, texts, cold contact) produced almost no response. The one clear success, Camellia Cleaning, worked because it came from an existing relationship and solved one concrete, felt problem — not because of a pitch.

Swoop (missed-call AI text-back) is structurally different: the pain is instantly understood by any home-service owner, requires no market education, and Sunil's own April 2026 brainstorm independently rated this exact idea (“Never Miss a Lead”) five stars — the top pick across 11 ideas evaluated.

DecisionPause the WelcomeMat homepage/positioning work. Put energy into finishing Swoop and getting it into a real pilot. Revisit WelcomeMat's broader positioning later, once there's a proven, trust-building win (like Camellia) to build from — not before.

2. Why Swoop, specifically

  • The pitch requires zero explanation: “we text your customer back the second you miss their call.”
  • Remaining work is a finite punch list (auth, call flow, compliance), not an open-ended identity question.
  • Priced for an easy yes relative to the pain of a lost $200–500 job.
  • Trust-building plays to Sunil's actual strength: hands-on discovery and concierge onboarding, same shape as the Camellia win.
  • Honest caveat: not uniquely differentiated technology — Podium, Housecall Pro, and others do versions of this. The wedge is price, simplicity, and personal onboarding, not novelty.

3. Pricing — reconsidered

Original plan: $29–49/mo. At 100 businesses, that's only ~$2,900–4,900/mo — not close to income replacement.

ReconsiderationSunil's own April brainstorm priced this same idea at $79–249/mo (tiered), benchmarked against real competitors charging $300–500/mo. The $29–49 range looks like an unexamined default rather than a deliberate choice. At $79–149/mo, 100 businesses = ~$95K–179K/year — a meaningfully different business.

Plan: Keep the pilot free (2–3 weeks, no commitment) regardless of eventual price. Use pilot data (calls missed, calls recovered, jobs booked) to set real pricing afterward, anchored to demonstrated ROI — not decided in advance.

4. Phasing

Phase 1 — Pilot-ready texting

Now
  • Close the known blockers: auth/business data isolation, forwarded-call flow (no second owner dial), A2P 10DLC + local number provisioning, compliance tests (STOP/START/HELP), safety/emergency phrasing, idempotency
  • Simplify the owner-facing console (see §5)
  • SMS-first reporting — weekly summary text, not a dashboard the owner has to check
  • Recruit 3–5 pilot businesses; run a 3–4 week controlled pilot
  • Goal: measure missed-call volume, reply rate, booked jobs, willingness to pay

Phase 2 — Prove and price

After pilot
  • Set real pricing from pilot ROI data, not guesswork
  • Identify which trade/vertical converted best — focus there next
  • Only expand dashboard features owners actually asked for during the pilot

Phase 3 — Voice

Later, contingent on Phase 1–2
  • Meaningfully bigger lift: latency targets, accent handling, barge-in, WA two-party consent disclosure
  • Worth building only once texting has paying customers and owners are asking for it
  • Natural future upsell toward a “Comms Hub” tier, not a Phase 1 requirement

6. Local numbers vs. toll-free — clearing up the confusion

The painful, weeks-long verification Sunil went through was for a toll-free number (TFN) — a separate, stricter path meant for high-volume outbound use. The actual pilot model doesn't need that: each business keeps its own public number and forwards only unanswered calls to one dedicated local Twilio number. That runs under a single A2P 10DLC brand + campaign registration (a one-time, days-not-weeks process) — after which provisioning additional local numbers under that same campaign is fast.
